## Bangladesh Bank Heist

The attackers broke into Bangladesh Bank's system and tried to attack through SWIFT, the secure messaging network for international financial transactions. They tried to take 1 billion dollars but only managed to get 81 million.

Before targeting the bank, the attackers remained in the network for an extended period of time and examined the details of the system on a regular basis. As a result, they were able to understand how the bank works, the SWIFT system, and how money transfers take place.

In this attack, the Integrity principle was abused the most. This is because the attackers not only stole money from the bank but also altered the bank’s transaction records and transfer instructions. 

The hackers attacked the SWIFT system to withdraw real transfer instructions and sabotaged the printer to print the bank’s confirmations of its transactions. This allowed them to delay the bank employees of seeing the transferred money right away. Delays in the response to the SWIFT attack are critical to the success of the hackers since it gives them more time to complete the transfers and conceal their tracks.

The attackers tried to withdraw about 1 billion dollars. Some transactions were flagged by the banks, and some withdrawals were suspicious. Moreover, small problems and mistakes stopped them. Small controls sometimes prevent big events.

I think stronger verification systems would help for Swift transactions. It would also be important to regularly check printers and transaction records.

Every attacker's goal is different; some try to steal data to blackmail, while others, like in this example, just go for the money and move on if they can get it.

I think defenders should question abnormally large money movements, and if I do this job in the future, I'll keep it in mind.
