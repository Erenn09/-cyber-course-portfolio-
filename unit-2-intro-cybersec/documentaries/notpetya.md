# NotPetya

NotPetya spread on June 27, 2017, through the update system of the Ukrainian accounting software M.E.Doc. It looked like ransomware, but its main job was to delete data. Within a few hours, it brought companies in many countries to a standstill.

The attack particularly affected institutions in Ukraine, and global giant companies that had partnerships with Ukrainian companies or shared the same network infrastructure were next in line. About 40,000 of Merck’s computers and servers became completely unusable. Global medicine production, research, and sales came to a standstill for weeks, causing roughly $1.4 billion in damages.

NotPetya tried with all its might to destroy the principle of availability, because its goal wasn't to steal or leak data but to make systems, infrastructures, and servers inaccessible and non-functional.

It was attacked through an accounting software called M.E.Doc, which Ukrainian companies and companies working with Ukrainian companies are required to use.

Instead of hacking systems one by one, attackers can take over the manufacturer's update server and spread malware to thousands or even millions of devices at the same time. And if automatic updates are on, the code gets loaded into the system automatically.

They found a computer on the network that wasn't updated and got in through the EternalBlue vulnerability, taking the admin passwords inside. Then they used the real passwords and logged into computers with security patches installed like legitimate users through Windows' built-in system tools. Even though the updated machines were protected from cybersecurity vulnerabilities, they couldn't resist the virus with its valid admin credentials.

Systems could be updated regularly and critical patches could be installed without delay. There could have been stricter authentication, meaning maybe a solution could have been found so that one admin password wouldn't allow taking over the entire network.

I think cyber attacks can cause a lot of damage because they are easily visible. For example, in this case Ukraine was being targeted, but big companies in other countries were also affected a lot.

The most important thing here is that even a software update that looks safe can be dangerous
