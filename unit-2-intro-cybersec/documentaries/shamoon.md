# Shamoon

In 2012 the Shamoon attack targeted Saudi Aramco. Deleted data from around 35,000 computers. This caused problems for the company because many computers stopped working and had to be replaced.

Saudi Aramco is a large oil company so the attack could affect more than just the company itself. Problems with its computers could affect oil operations, suppliers and the wider energy market.

The main CIA principles attacked were **availability and integrity**. The attackers destroyed data. Made computers unusable which meant employees could not access important systems and files.

Shamoon spread across computers and used a wiper to destroy data. There was also a part in how the attackers got access to the network. Unlike ransomware a wiper is mainly designed to destroy data than encrypt it and ask for money.

Saudi Aramco had to get thousands of hard drives and work quickly to restore its systems. The incident showed that recovery can be very expensive and time‑consuming when a company does not have prepared replacement equipment.

Better **network segmentation** could have reduced the damage by separating systems from each other. This could make it harder for malware to spread to thousands of computers at once.

The attack shows that critical infrastructure can be targeted when the goal is not stealing money or information. Energy companies, hospitals and transport systems are especially important because an attack could also affect life and other organizations.

The episode made me think more, about how important backupsre. If a company loses thousands of computers at once having backups and a recovery plan can make a huge difference.
